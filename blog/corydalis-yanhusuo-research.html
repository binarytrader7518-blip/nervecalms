Corydalis Yanhusuo: What the Research Says About This Root

Corydalis yanhusuo is the first ingredient the seller lists in NerveCalm. Learn about its traditional use, its alkaloids and what research shows so far.

By the NerveCalm Editorial TeamUpdated November 23, 20265 min read
In this article
  1. A long traditional history
  2. What makes it interesting to researchers
  3. How strong is the evidence?
  4. Safety notes

Corydalis yanhusuo, often called yanhusuo or yuanhu, is a flowering plant in the poppy family. Its tuber has been used in traditional Chinese herbal practice for centuries and is the first ingredient the seller lists for NerveCalm.

A long traditional history

According to a 2021 review published in Molecules and archived by the National Library of Medicine, Corydalis extracts were first documented in a classical Chinese text more than a thousand years ago. Traditionally, they were used to ease different kinds of discomfort.

What makes it interesting to researchers

Corydalis contains a large family of compounds called alkaloids. A 2023 review in Biomedicine & Pharmacotherapy describes 84 alkaloids isolated from the plant. One of the best known is dehydrocorybulbine (DHCB), which researchers have studied in animal models of discomfort.

How strong is the evidence?

It's important to read this research honestly:

  • Most findings come from laboratory and animal studies, not large human clinical trials.
  • Research on an isolated compound or extract doesn't prove that a finished multi-ingredient product works the same way.
  • Dose, extraction method and plant part all matter. The NerveCalm seller does not publish how much Corydalis each capsule contains.

In other words, Corydalis is a well-studied traditional botanical with promising early research. It is not a proven treatment.

Safety notes

Like many potent herbs, Corydalis may not suit everyone. People who are pregnant or nursing, have liver concerns, or take sedatives or other medications should talk with a healthcare professional first. NCCIH's guide to using dietary supplements wisely is a good place to start.
