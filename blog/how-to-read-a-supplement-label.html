How to Read a Supplement Label Before You Buy

A step-by-step guide to reading supplement labels: serving size, proprietary blends, other ingredients, warnings and what claims can legally say.

A supplement label tells you far more than the front of the bottle. Here is how to read one, using NerveCalm as a worked example.

1. The front panel

The NerveCalm bottle shows the product name, "Food Supplement in Capsules," the tagline "Nerve Calming Formula," four featured botanicals (marshmallow root, Corydalis powder, passion flower and California poppy) and the capsule count: 60. Front panels are marketing, so always turn the bottle around.

2. The Supplement Facts panel

This is where you'll find the serving size, servings per container, and each ingredient with its amount. For NerveCalm, the seller's directions are two capsules a day, so 60 capsules equals 30 servings.

3. Proprietary blends

Some products group ingredients into a named "blend" and list only the total weight. That's legal, but it means you can't see how much of each ingredient you're getting. The NerveCalm seller lists blend totals on its website but no per-ingredient amounts, so the bottle's panel is the place to check.

4. Other ingredients

Look for capsule material (gelatin or plant-based), fillers and allergens. This matters if you're vegetarian or have sensitivities.

5. Claims and the disclaimer

In the US, supplements may make "structure/function" claims, such as "supports nerve comfort," but not disease claims like "cures neuropathy." Structure/function claims must carry the FDA disclaimer stating that they haven't been evaluated by the FDA. Be cautious with any product promising guaranteed or overnight results.

6. Dates and storage

NerveCalm's seller says to use each bottle within 24 months of the manufacture date stamped on it. Store supplements somewhere cool and dry, out of reach of children.

Quick checklist

  • Is the serving size clear?
  • Are ingredient amounts disclosed?
  • Are there allergens or fillers you want to avoid?
  • Do the claims sound realistic?
  • Is there a clear refund policy? NerveCalm's seller offers a 90-day money-back guarantee.
